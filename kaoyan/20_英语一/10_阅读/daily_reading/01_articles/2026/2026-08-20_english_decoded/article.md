LEARNING A FOREIGN LANGUAGE is a lot of fun but also quite challenging – not helped by the fact that people communicate in very different ways. [!] Millions of people across the world speak English, so providing rules about how people communicate specifically in English is difficult. [!] That said, English, like every language, has nuanced ways of sending certain messages. And that is our focus here. ^p01

We’ll help you appreciate the difference between what people say and what they actually mean in English – in many different situations. First, look at the box on page 13 to learn about three types of communicators. Then, move on to the quiz to see examples of what each type might typically say in English in a certain scenario – and, more importantly, to find out how well you can read between the lines. ^p02

## Styles of communication

Assertive communicators [?] have mastered the art of being “firm but fair”. They stand up for their rights and set clear boundaries, while still being respectful of other people. Assertive communication tends to be pleasant and straightforward. ^p03

Aggressive communicators are straight talkers, who let people know exactly how they feel. They can sound aggressive and often cause unnecessary upset – but at least they get their point across and you’ll know where you stand with them. ^p04

Passive-aggressive communicators avoid direct conflict. They use sarcasm to express their feelings or say the opposite of what they mean – a style of communication that only works, of course, if the other person gets the hidden message. ^p05

Would you be able to recognize, let’s say, the sarcasm in someone’s seemingly helpful response? Would you fall for a friendly offer that’s actually not an offer at all but a reproach? Would you realize that your conversation partner is, in fact, not as understanding as he or she sounds? ^p06

## Read between the lines!

Here are ten everyday situations and a choice of three responses for each one. Which response should put you on your guard – and best not be taken literally? Take a moment to consider what your conversation partner is really saying, then tick the box. ^p07

### 1. A colleague is already struggling with their own work

You ask a colleague for help, but they are already struggling with their own work and say: ^p08

- A. “That’s not my responsibility and I’m busy.” (straight )
- B. “I can’t do anything today, but if you make a start, I could find some time to help you on Friday, if you still need it.”(kind and will)
- C. “Sure. I guess I could do your work as well as my own. Why not?” (sarcasm)^p09

### 2. Dirty dishes in the sink

You have left a pile of dirty dishes in the sink. Your housemate says: ^p10

- A. “I’ll just put my rubber gloves on and wash these few dishes. It’s not a big deal, is it?”(direct to critize with anger)
- B. “I’ve noticed there are some dirty dishes in the sink. Could you wash them by bedtime, so everything is clean for the end of the day?”(kind request)
- C. “You need to wash these dishes.”(straight) ^p11

### 3. An upcoming deadline

You remind a staff member of an upcoming deadline. They say: ^p12

- A. “Don’t worry. I have it on my schedule for Thursday. I’ll let you know if I hit any problems, otherwise, it’s all in hand.” [?] (confident let me believe)
- B. “I know. It’s in my calendar. I don’t need you to micromanage me.”(dont bother me)
- C. “Oh, I’m sorry I haven’t already sent the work in ahead of the deadline. Would you like me to send you daily updates?” (kind ) ^p13

### 4. Jumping the queue

You’re in a terrible rush and give in to the temptation to jump the queue. Someone says: ^p14

- A. “Excuse me! There’s a queue here.”(staight)
- B. “No, please, go right ahead! After you!”(annoyed and sarcasm)
- C. “Hey! You’re pushing in! Get to the back of the queue like everyone else!”(direct) ^p15

### 5. Unasked-for career advice

With the best intentions, you volunteer career advice to a much younger family member. [!] They say: ^p16

- A. “Thank you for your opinion. I’ll be sure to bear it in mind.”(kind)
- B. “Your advice isn’t helpful because you’re out of touch.”(antipathy)
- C. “I appreciate that you’re looking out for me, but things have changed in the last few years and I’m confident in my decisions.”(kind to refuse) ^p17

### 6. A dog barking late at night

Your dog is barking late at night, keeping everyone awake. A neighbour says: ^p18

- A. (shouting out of the window) “Shut that dog up or I’m calling the police!”(annoyed and straight)
- B. (posting on a neighbourhood app) “I’m worried for the wellbeing of the dog – and its owner. Is something wrong?”(sarcasm)
- C. (in person) “I wanted to talk to you about your dog. Is there something you can do to settle him down at night?” (kind to request)^p19

### 7. Interrupting a colleague in a meeting

In a meeting, you have a brilliant idea and can’t stop yourself from interrupting the colleague who’s currently speaking. She says: ^p20

- A. “I’ll just be quiet – you clearly have something important to say.”(sarcasm)
- B. “Will you shut up and let me finish for once?”(straight)
- C. “Hang on a second, I’d like to finish my thought before we move on to your point.” (kind) ^p21
### 8. Cancelling plans repeatedly

You have had to cancel plans with a friend a few times, sometimes at the very last minute. He says: ^p22

- A. “You’re very unreliable. I don’t want to make any more plans with you.”(straight)
- B. “I’m sure we’ll eventually manage to find a date you can do, if your busy schedule allows.”(unpleasant sarcasm)
- C. “I’ve noticed we’ve had to cancel a few things lately. I’d really like to see you, but it feels rather one-sided at the moment.”(kind and sad)^p23

### 9. Looking at your phone while your partner talks

You are reading the news on your phone while your partner is trying to tell you about their day. Your partner says: ^p24

- A. “Put that phone down! You never listen to a word I say.”(annoyed and complaint)
- B. “I’d really like to chat. Can you put the phone down?”(direct)
- C. “I’ll call my mum instead. She’s always interested in what I’ve been doing.”(straight and a bit of sarcasm) ^p25

### 10. Not thanking someone who holds the door

Someone holds the door open for you and, completely lost in thought, you don’t thank them. They say: ^p26

- A. “You’re welcome.”(sarcasm)
- B. “That was rude!”(direct)
- C. Nothing. Life’s too short...(sarcasm?) ^p27

## What is said – and what is meant

Here are some classic passive-aggressive phrases – complete with a guide to their (not so) secret hidden meanings: ^p28

- “As you are aware, …” = I suspect you haven’t got a clue what I’m talking about.
- “For future reference, …” = Don’t make the same silly mistake again!
- “As I stated in my last email, …” = You either didn’t read or didn’t understand my last email.
- “A gentle reminder that…” = You never stick to your deadlines!
- “Don’t worry about me!” = You should soooo worry – I need your undivided attention right now!
- “I guess I’ll just do it myself.” = You’re not going to do it anyway – or not well enough. In fact, you never help me!
- “I have nothing better to do with my time.” = I’m incredibly busy, as usual, but no one cares, as usual.
- “Better late than never!” = How difficult can it be to be on time? You’re always late!
- “I felt a bit silly.” = I was made to feel a complete idiot because of you! You’d better apologize – and a bunch of flowers won’t be enough! [?]
- “It’s not your fault, really!” = Of course it’s your fault! Who else’s fault would it be?
- “My mistake entirely!” = You got it wrong! How could you possibly make such a dumb mistake?
- “This really is quite tricky!” = How can you not understand something so simple? ^p29
